## Quotes & evidence

### Q1 — Definition of phronesis (NE VI.5)

> "We may grasp the nature of practical wisdom if we consider what sort of people we call practically wise… It is the mark of a man of practical wisdom to be able to deliberate well about what is good and expedient for himself, not in some particular respect, e.g. about what sorts of thing conduce to health or to strength, but about what sorts of thing conduce to the good life in general."
>
> "Practical wisdom, then, must be a reasoned and true state of capacity to act with regard to the things that are good or bad for man."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.5, **1140a24–b6, b4–6**, W.D. Ross translation (Internet Classics Archive). Greek key term: φρόνησις. The Loeb (Rackham) renders the definition as "a truth-attaining rational quality, concerned with action in relation to things that are good and bad for human beings."

**Context**: Book VI catalogues the five "intellectual virtues" of the rational part of the soul — *episteme* (scientific knowledge), *techne* (art/craft), *phronesis* (practical wisdom), *sophia* (philosophical wisdom), *nous* (intuitive reason). Chapter 5 isolates phronesis by reverse-engineering from those we already call wise in practice.
**Translation (ko)**: "실천적 지혜(프로네시스)는 인간에게 좋은 것과 나쁜 것에 관해 이성에 따라 행위할 수 있는 참된 품성 상태(헥시스)이다." (통상적 한국어 등가; 강상진·이창우·김재홍 역 어휘 기준)

---

### Q2 — Pericles as example of phronimos (NE VI.5)

> "This is why we think Pericles and men of that stamp have practical wisdom, viz. because they can see what is good for themselves and what is good for men in general; we consider that those can do this who are good at managing households or states."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.5, **≈ 1140b7–11**, W.D. Ross trans. (Chase: "we think Pericles and men of that stamp to be Practically Wise, because they can see what is good for themselves and for men in general").

**Context**: Right after the definition. Aristotle anchors the abstract definition by naming a paradigmatic public exemplar — the Athenian statesman Pericles — and by extending the title to those who govern *oikoi* (households) well, not just polities.
**Translation (ko)**: "우리가 페리클레스 같은 부류의 사람을 실천적 지혜를 가진 자라고 보는 까닭은, 그들이 자기에게 좋은 것과 일반적으로 인간에게 좋은 것을 볼 수 있기 때문이다."

### Q3 — Phronesis vs. techne and episteme (NE VI.5, VI.3–4)

> "Now it is thought to be the mark of a man of practical wisdom to be able to deliberate well… But no one deliberates about things that are invariable, nor about things that it is impossible for him to do. Therefore, since scientific knowledge involves demonstration, but there is no demonstration of things whose first principles are variable… it remains that it is a true and reasoned state of capacity to act with regard to the things that are good or bad for man… Further, while there is such a thing as excellence in art, there is no such thing as excellence in practical wisdom; and in art he who errs willingly is preferable, but in practical wisdom, as in the virtues, he is the reverse."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.5, **1140a24–1140b30**, Ross trans.

**Context**: Aristotle explicitly distinguishes three things:
- *episteme* (1139b18–24): demonstrative, about the eternal/necessary.
- *techne* (1140a1–23): productive (*poiesis*), aims at an end outside the activity (the made object).
- *phronesis* (1140a24–b30): practical (*praxis*), its end is acting well itself; concerns the contingent and variable.

**Translation (ko)**: "프로네시스는 학적 지식(에피스테메)도 기술(테크네)도 아니다. 학적 지식은 변하지 않는 것을 다루고, 기술은 자신과 다른 산출물을 목적으로 삼지만, 프로네시스는 변할 수 있는 인간사 속에서의 행위 자체를 다루며 그 자체가 목적이다."

### Q4 — Universals and particulars (NE VI.7)

> "Practical wisdom is not concerned with universals only — it must also recognize the particulars; for it is practical, and practice is concerned with particulars. This is why some who do not know, and especially those who have experience, are more practical than others who know."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.7, **1141b14–18** (Ross trans., MIT Classics).

**Context**: Aristotle is arguing against a purely theoretical conception of moral knowledge. He gives an example: someone who knows that light meats are digestible and healthy but doesn't know that chicken is a light meat will fail to act well; the person who knows the particular case (chicken is light) acts well. Hence the practical superiority of the experienced over the merely learned.
**Translation (ko)**: "프로네시스는 보편적인 것만이 아니라 개별적인 것도 알아야 한다. 행위는 개별적인 것들에 관한 것이기 때문이다. 그래서 학적 지식이 없어도 경험이 있는 사람이 더 잘 행위하는 일이 있다."

---

### Q5 — Young men cannot be phronimoi (NE VI.8)

> "What has been said is confirmed by the fact that while young men become geometricians and mathematicians and wise in matters like these, it is thought that a young man of practical wisdom cannot be found. The cause is that such wisdom is concerned not only with universals but with particulars, which become familiar from experience, but a young man has no experience, for it is length of time that gives experience."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.8, **1142a11–20** (Ross trans.). Chase: "the young come to be geometricians, and mathematicians, and Scientific in such matters, but it is not thought that a young man can come to be possessed of Practical Wisdom."

**Context**: Aristotle's signature contrast between theoretical disciplines (acquirable young because their objects are universals one can grasp through proof) and practical wisdom (requires accumulated exposure to particular human situations).
**Translation (ko)**: "젊은이도 기하학자나 수학자, 그런 종류의 학자는 될 수 있어도 프로네시스를 갖춘 사람은 될 수 없다. 프로네시스는 보편만이 아니라 경험에서 익숙해지는 개별을 다루는데, 젊은이에게는 시간이 만들어내는 경험이 없기 때문이다."

### Q6 — Virtue makes the goal right; phronesis makes the means right (NE VI.12)

> "Virtue makes us aim at the right mark, and practical wisdom makes us take the right means." (Ross)
>
> Alternative formulation: "Man's work as Man is accomplished by virtue of Practical Wisdom and Moral Virtue, the latter giving the right aim and direction, the former the right means to its attainment." (Chase)
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.12, **1144a6–9** (Ross). Greek formulation often cited: "ἡ μὲν γὰρ ἀρετὴ τὸν σκοπὸν ποιεῖ ὀρθόν, ἡ δὲ φρόνησις τὰ πρὸς τοῦτον" (1144a7–8).

**Context**: A central crux. Moss (NYU paper) reads this as: ethical virtue sets the *telos* (the right end), phronesis figures out *ta pros to telos* — the things conducive to it. The relationship is bidirectional in VI.12–13: without virtue the deliberator is merely "clever" (*deinotes*), without phronesis the virtuous person is merely "naturally virtuous" and not fully virtuous.
**Translation (ko)**: "덕은 목표를 올바르게 만들고, 프로네시스는 그 목표에 이르는 것들을 올바르게 만든다."

---

### Q7 — Phronesis vs. mere cleverness (deinotes) (NE VI.12)

> Phronesis "is not the same as this capacity [cleverness], although it involves it… If the mark is noble, the cleverness is laudable, but if the mark is base, the cleverness is mere villainy; hence we call clever both men of practical wisdom and villains."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.12, **1144a23–29** (Ross trans., paraphrased through Stanford Encyclopedia + Jessica Moss).

**Context**: Aristotle separates *deinotes* (δεινότης) — the bare instrumental skill of getting from goal to goal — from *phronesis*. A clever villain has *deinotes* but not *phronesis*, because phronesis has a built-in orientation to the genuinely good.
**Translation (ko)**: "영리함(데이노테스)은 어떤 목표든 효과적으로 달성하는 능력이지만, 프로네시스가 아니다. 목표가 고귀하면 영리함은 칭찬받고, 비루하면 그것은 교활함이다. 프로네시스는 영리함을 포함하지만 그것에 머무르지 않는다."

---

### Q8 — No real virtue without phronesis (NE VI.13)

> "It is clear, then, from what has been said that it is not possible to be good in the strict sense without practical wisdom, nor practically wise without moral virtue."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.13, **1144b30–1145a2** (Ross trans.).

> Earlier in the same chapter, Aristotle distinguishes "natural virtue" (φυσικὴ ἀρετή) from "virtue in the strict sense" (κυρίως ἀρετή): the former is a temperamental disposition we are born with, the latter requires phronesis. "Ethical virtue is fully developed only when it is combined with practical wisdom" (1144b14–17, cited via Stanford Encyclopedia).

**Context**: The famous "unity of the virtues" argument. The phronimos and the virtuous person are co-constituted — neither can exist without the other. This is also the source of the classical "circularity" objection (Q15 below).
**Translation (ko)**: "엄밀한 의미로 좋은 사람은 프로네시스 없이 있을 수 없고, 프로네시스를 가진 사람은 성품의 덕(에티케 아레테) 없이 있을 수 없다."

---

### Q9 — Phronesis as deliberation about the variable / contingent (NE VI.5, VI.7)

> "No one deliberates about things that are invariable, nor about things that it is impossible for him to do. Therefore… practical wisdom cannot be scientific knowledge [episteme]… nor art [techne]." (NE VI.5, ≈1140a31–b4, Ross)

**Context**: Phronesis is constitutively about τὰ ἐνδεχόμενα ἄλλως ἔχειν — "things that admit of being otherwise." Contrast with sophia/episteme which concern the necessary and eternal.
**Translation (ko)**: "달리 될 수 없는 것에 대해서는 숙고하지 않는다. 프로네시스는 달리 될 수 있는 것에 관한 숙고이다."

---

### Q10 — Sophia is "higher" than phronesis (NE VI.7, VI.13; X.7)

> "It is strange if someone thinks that politics or practical wisdom is the most excellent kind of knowledge, unless man is the best thing in the cosmos."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.7, **1141a20–22** (Stanford Encyclopedia translation).

**Context**: Aristotle explicitly ranks *sophia* (theoretical wisdom, contemplation of the eternal) above *phronesis*. Phronesis governs human affairs; sophia contemplates the divine/unchanging. This sets up the tension that EE VIII.1 and NE X.7–8 keep wrestling with: is the *bios theoretikos* (contemplative life) the highest, with phronesis serving it, or is phronesis architectonic in its own domain?
**Translation (ko)**: "만일 인간이 우주에서 최고의 존재가 아니라면, 정치학이나 프로네시스가 최고의 학문이라고 생각하는 것은 이상하다."

---

### Q11 — The medical / navigation analogy (NE II.2)

> "Matters concerned with conduct and what is good for us have no fixity, any more than matters of health… the agents themselves must in each case consider what is appropriate to the situation, as happens in the art (techne) of medicine and navigation."
>
> — *Nicomachean Ethics* II.2, **≈ 1104a3–10** (commonly cited via Ross / Vaia summary).

**Context**: Aristotle's recurring analogy — medicine and seamanship — to illustrate why ethics cannot achieve mathematical precision. Note: Aristotle classifies medicine itself as *techne*, not *phronesis*; but later interpreters (Pellegrino, Kaldjian, Kinghorn, Schwartz) argue clinical decision-making at the bedside *requires* phronesis because each patient is a particular. This is itself a contested move (see Q17).
**Translation (ko)**: "행위와 인간에게 좋은 것에 관한 일은 건강의 일처럼 고정성이 없다. 행위자는 매번 상황에 적절한 것을 의술이나 항해술의 경우처럼 스스로 살펴야 한다."

### Q12 — Eudemian Ethics VIII.1, 1246b on phronesis

> [Stănilă 2016 summary]: "EE VIII.1, 1246b.4–1246b.36 is a highly corrupted text dealing with the subject of phronesis in the *Eudemian Ethics*… Phronesis is the capability of truly understanding the end of human life and also of discerning the appropriate means to attain this end."
>
> — Cited via Stănilă, "Aristotle's Treatment of φρόνησις in *Eudemian Ethics*, VIII, 1," *Philobiblon*.

**Context**: The *Eudemian Ethics* passage is textually corrupt but is widely read as endorsing the same dual structure as NE VI.12 — phronesis grasps both end and means (or at least the means-route to the end set by ethical virtue). The corruption is itself a fact of the textual tradition worth noting; modern editions diverge.
**Translation (ko)**: "에우데모스 윤리학 VIII권 1장(1246b.4–36)은 본문 자체가 심하게 훼손된 단락이지만, 프로네시스가 인간 삶의 목적을 참되게 파악하고 그 목적에 이르는 적절한 수단도 분별하는 능력이라는 동일한 구도가 등장한다."

### Q13 — Joseph Dunne on phronesis vs. techne (1993)

> [Dunne, *Back to the Rough Ground*, summarised by publisher and reviewers]: "Dunne examines the two central categories in Aristotle's own texts, emphasising the theoretical inclination of *techne* and the inescapably personal and experiential nature of *phronesis*… [the book] develops a philosophical context for exposing the limits of technical reason and for analysing the practical knowledge that informs successful activity in different domains."
>
> — Joseph Dunne, *Back to the Rough Ground: Practical Judgment and the Lure of Technique* (Notre Dame Press, 1993). Foreword by Alasdair MacIntyre. Description via Everand and Goodreads.

**Context**: Dunne is the canonical modern restoration of the phronesis/techne distinction. He traces the recovery of phronesis through Newman, Collingwood, Arendt, Gadamer, and Habermas. The "rough ground" of the title is Wittgenstein's image — friction, particularity, irreducibility — which Dunne maps onto phronesis against the "lure of technique" (the modern push to algorithmise everything).
**Translation (ko)**: "테크네는 이론화 가능한 일반 절차에 가까운 반면, 프로네시스는 사람과 경험에 환원 불가능하게 매여 있다. Dunne의 책은 '테크닉의 유혹'(모든 것을 알고리즘화하려는 현대적 충동)에 맞서 phronesis의 '거친 땅'을 복권하려는 시도이다."

### Q14 — Gadamer: phronesis as model for hermeneutic application

> [Stanford Encyclopedia on Gadamer]: "The knowledge appropriate for the hermeneutic task is what Aristotle names as 'phronesis,' which requires application but not in the way that modern science understands application as a secondary and discrete step that follows from prior theoretical knowledge."
>
> [Rosenberg, "The Place of Phronesis in Gadamer's Hermeneutics"]: "application is not a separate, subsequent stage after understanding and interpretation; it is internal to them — understanding a text involves grasping what it says here and now."
>
> — Hans-Georg Gadamer, *Truth and Method* (1960), Part II.II.2.b "The Hermeneutical Problem and the Aristotelian Distinction" (Bekker-equivalent location in Gadamer's exposition).

**Context**: Gadamer's most influential post-war move: phronesis is not just an item in Aristotle's ethics but the *paradigm* for all interpretive understanding. The judge reading a statute, the doctor reading a patient, the reader interpreting a text — all are doing phronesis, not techne.
**Translation (ko)**: "Gadamer에게 텍스트 이해(해석학적 행위)는 본질적으로 프로네시스이다. '적용'은 이해 다음에 오는 별도 단계가 아니라 이해 그 자체에 내재한다."

### Q15 — MacIntyre: phronesis lost in modernity (*After Virtue*, 1981)

> [Jubilee Centre paper, summarising MacIntyre]: "Phronesis is the intellectual virtue that makes judgment possible, and is different both from the modern expert and from the modern untrained individual… without this virtue, none of the other virtues can really be exercised, as the same action which would in one situation be liberality could in another be prodigality and in a third meanness."
>
> [DBU outline of *After Virtue*]: "MacIntyre's diagnosis is that modern moral philosophy has not delivered a stable, rational account of why moral rules should bind us, connecting that failure to the abandonment of Aristotelian teleology — the idea that human beings have a characteristic end that practical reason can identify and that virtues help us reach… Interest in phronesis gradually faded in Enlightenment and post-Enlightenment theorising, with phronesis being brushed off as both too moralistic and indeterminate as a decision procedure."
>
> — Alasdair MacIntyre, *After Virtue* (Notre Dame Press, 1981/2007), Ch. 14 "The Nature of the Virtues."

**Context**: MacIntyre's claim: post-Enlightenment moral philosophy abandoned both *telos* (the goal-directedness of human life) and the *traditions* that nurture phronesis, leaving rule-following or sentiment as substitutes. Phronesis is intelligible only inside a "practice" with its own goods and a tradition that hands them down.
**Translation (ko)**: "MacIntyre는 근대 도덕철학이 텔로스(목적)와 그것을 전수하는 전통을 잃은 채 규칙 또는 감정으로 도덕을 재구성하려 했고, 그 과정에서 프로네시스가 '너무 도덕주의적이고 결정 절차로는 너무 모호하다'는 이유로 사라졌다고 진단한다."

### Q16 — Nussbaum: phronesis as perception of particulars (*Fragility of Goodness*, 1986)

> [Nussbaum, summarised via *Fragility of Goodness* Ch. 10 "Non-Scientific Deliberation" and Ch. 13]: "human emotions are an important element of phronesis because they enable us to see features of a situation that our cognitive intellect alone cannot, functioning as 'discriminating modes of perception, responsive to the workings of deliberation and essential to its completion.'"
>
> "Aristotle prioritizes the concrete individual case — such as the unique demands of friendship or justice in a specific conflict — because universals alone cannot capture the nuances that luck introduces into human affairs."
>
> — Martha Nussbaum, *The Fragility of Goodness*, Cambridge UP (1986), esp. Chs. 10 and 13.

**Context**: Nussbaum's central contribution is to argue that phronesis includes the emotions as cognitive — they are not noise interfering with deliberation but themselves a form of perception of the morally salient.
**Translation (ko)**: "Nussbaum은 프로네시스가 단순한 추론이 아니라 '개별 상황을 식별하는 지각(perception)'이며, 감정 역시 그 지각의 일부라고 본다. 보편 규칙만으로는 운(tyche)이 끼어드는 인간사의 미묘함을 포착할 수 없다."

### Q17 — Phronesis revival in medicine (Pellegrino, Kaldjian, Kinghorn)

> [Malik, Conroy & Turner 2020, *Health Care Analysis*]: "Phronesis – or practical wisdom – is a key concept in the field of virtue ethics, and it has played a significant role in the thinking of those who make use of virtue when theorising medical practice and ethics."
>
> "Kaldjian's five-stage theoretical framework: goals, concrete circumstances, virtues, deliberation and motivation to act."

### Q18 — Schwartz & Sharpe: practical wisdom as antidote to rule-and-incentive culture

> [Schwartz & Sharpe, *Practical Wisdom*, 2010, publisher description]: "Aristotle thought ethics meant figuring out the right way to do the right thing in a particular circumstance, with a particular person, at a particular time."
>
> "The authors draw on Aristotle as their source… Society needs the Aristotelian ideal that trumps all others — practical wisdom."
>
> — Barry Schwartz & Kenneth Sharpe, *Practical Wisdom: The Right Way to Do the Right Thing* (Riverhead, 2010).

**Context**: Schwartz/Sharpe popularise phronesis as the missing ingredient in modern professions over-regulated by rules and over-motivated by incentives. Their canonical example: a hospital janitor who skipped his cleaning schedule to comfort a grieving family — that's phronesis, and no protocol captures it.
**Translation (ko)**: "Schwartz와 Sharpe는 '규칙'과 '인센티브'에 의존하는 현대 조직이 phronesis(실천적 지혜)를 압살하고 있다고 주장하며, 아리스토텔레스의 phronesis 부활을 제안한다."

### Q19 — Heidegger's appropriation and its critics

> [Weidenfeld 2011, abstract]: "Heidegger's phenomenology is an appropriation of Aristotle's practical philosophy and his conceptualization of phronesis. In *Being and Time*, Heidegger's 'circumspection' [Umsicht], which is a capacity for making sense of practical situations, is a translation of phronesis."
>
> [Gillespie, cited via Weidenfeld]: "Heidegger's Aristotelianism was tied directly to his Nazism… Heidegger's translation of phronesis removed its rational content and transformed the concept into a form of 'decision by mystical inspiration.'"
>
> — Matthew C. Weidenfeld, "Heidegger's Appropriation of Aristotle," *European Journal of Political Theory* 10(2): 254–276 (2011); referencing Michael Allen Gillespie.

**Context**: A serious cautionary tale in the phronesis revival literature. Heidegger took phronesis as a model for *Entschlossenheit* (resoluteness), which Gillespie and others read as dropping the *logos* (the reasoned, communicable character of phronesis) and keeping only the decisive moment — a move that aligned uncomfortably with his Nazi politics.
**Translation (ko)**: "Heidegger는 phronesis를 '결단(Entschlossenheit)'으로 번역해 사용했는데, Gillespie 등은 이것이 phronesis에서 이성적·공동체적 logos를 떼어내고 '신비적 영감에 의한 결단'으로 변질시켰다고 비판한다. 이는 그의 나치 정치와 무관하지 않다."

### Q20 — Feminist / elitism critique

> [Cornell dissertation, "Aristotle on the Virtues of Slaves, Women, and Children"; *Eugesta* review of Aristotle *Politics* 1252a–1260b]: "Some scholars argue women should exercise practical wisdom (phronēsis) in domestic roles, yet Aristotle excludes them from civic deliberation. Wives can weigh 'ethical particulars' in domestic life but are excluded from the architectonic prudence (phronēsis) needed for civic deliberation… Women possess deliberative reason but 'lack authority,' a condition that legitimises the husband's permanent rule while distinguishing wives from slaves, who 'wholly lack' reason."
>
> — Aristotle, *Politics* I.13, 1260a12–14 (commonly cited locus): "The slave wholly lacks the deliberative faculty (τὸ βουλευτικόν); the female has it, but it lacks authority (ἄκυρον); the child has it, but it is undeveloped (ἀτελές)."

**Context**: The most-cited internal evidence for the elitism charge. Aristotle's phronesis-bearing class is, by his own architecture, the adult free male citizen. Modern revivers (Schwartz, Kaldjian, Nussbaum, Kristjánsson) must either reject this restriction (Nussbaum, Kristjánsson explicitly do) or excise it as a historically contingent error.
**Translation (ko)**: "아리스토텔레스의 phronesis는 자유시민·성숙한 남성에게만 온전히 가능한 것으로 설계되어 있다(*정치학* 1260a12–14: 노예는 숙고력 자체가 없고, 여성은 있지만 '권위가 없고(akyron)', 아이는 '미성숙하다(ateles)'). 이는 현대적 부활 시 반드시 다뤄야 할 비판이다."

### Q21 — The circularity objection

> [*Phronesis* Pressbooks textbook chapter on virtue ethics]: "An objection to Aristotelian Virtue Ethics is based on a concern regarding logical circularity. Aristotle defines phronêsis by reference to the practically wise person, ho phronimos, someone who has phronêsis. This creates a definitional circularity problem — virtue is defined by reference to the virtuous person, and the virtuous person is identified by their possession of phronesis."
>
> — Aristotelian Virtue Ethics chapter, https://pressbooks.pub/phronesis/chapter/virtue-ethics/

**Context**: The classic "how do we identify the phronimos in the first place?" worry. Aristotle's text relies on ostensive identification ("people like Pericles") and on the inseparability thesis (virtue and phronesis co-arise). Critics charge this is circular; defenders (Moss, McDowell) argue the circle is virtuous, not vicious — like the circle in identifying the expert in any practice.
**Translation (ko)**: "프로네시스는 phronimos(실천적 지혜를 가진 자)에 의해 정의되고, phronimos는 phronesis를 가진 자로 정의된다는 순환성 문제. Aristotle은 페리클레스 같은 모범을 가리키는 ostensive definition으로 이를 부분 해소하지만, 비판자들은 여전히 결정 절차로서 phronesis가 '누가 진짜 phronimos인가'를 식별하는 기준을 제공하지 못한다고 본다."

### Q22 — Phronesis vs. algorithm / AI

> [Vallor & others, *Moral Philosophy and Politics* and *AI and Ethics*]: "AI can tell you what happened and predict what might happen based on historical patterns, but it can't replace practical wisdom… A significant concern is that the growing prevalence of statistical machine learning in everyday decision making effectively replaces many of our humdrum practical judgments and will eventually undermine our capacity for making such judgments."
>
> "Phronesis is not an abstract capacity that can be generalized across contexts… AI can't tell you what you should do about it when the situation involves competing values, uncertain outcomes."
>
> — Composite: Vallor (?) "AI and Phronesis" *Moral Philosophy and Politics* (de Gruyter 2021); separate article in *AI and Ethics* (Springer 2024) "Why AI may undermine phronesis and what to do about it." https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43681-024-00617-0

**Context**: Contemporary debate. One camp (Floridi, narrow ML proponents) treats ethical decision-making as in principle automatable. Another (Vallor, Dunne-influenced) argues phronesis is structurally non-algorithmic because (a) it requires perception of the particular, (b) it requires a stake in the *telos*, (c) it co-evolves with the person's character — none of which an algorithm has.
**Translation (ko)**: "AI/알고리즘이 best-practice 결정을 대체할 수 있다는 입장과, phronesis는 (개별 상황 지각·목적에 대한 이해관계·인격 형성과 함께 발달한다는 점에서) 구조적으로 알고리즘화 불가능하다는 입장이 맞선다."

## Recurring observations (raw, 해석 금지)

- "particulars / individuals / the variable / 개별"이 거의 모든 1차·2차 자료에서 phronesis의 핵심 표지로 반복된다 (Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q9, Q11, Q16, Q17, Q23).
- "experience / 경험 / length of time / 시간"이 phronesis 형성의 필수 조건으로 반복된다 (Q4, Q5).
- "deliberation / 숙고 / 카탈리쥐 / *bouleuesthai*"가 phronesis의 핵심 활동으로 반복된다 (Q1, Q9, Q12, Q17, Q20).
- "ends vs. means / 목표와 수단 / 텔로스와 그것에 이르는 것들"의 분업 구도가 NE VI.12, EE VIII.1, Moss 해석, MacIntyre, Schwartz 모두에서 동일하게 등장한다 (Q6, Q12, Q15, Q18).
- "no virtue without phronesis & no phronesis without virtue"의 inseparability thesis가 1차 텍스트와 모든 2차 자료에서 반복된다 (Q6, Q7, Q8, Q15, Q21).
- "techne / episteme / sophia"에 대한 negative definition (phronesis는 X가 아니다)이 거의 모든 자료에서 정의 방법으로 사용된다 (Q1, Q3, Q9, Q10, Q11, Q13).
- "Pericles", "doctor / medicine", "navigator / helmsman"가 phronesis 예시로 반복 출현한다 (Q2, Q11, Q17).
- "cleverness (deinotes)"와 phronesis의 구분이 NE VI.12와 Moss·Stanford 백과사전에서 강조된다 (Q7, Q15).
- "rules / algorithms / protocols 의 한계"라는 표현이 modern 자료에서 일관되게 등장한다 (Q13, Q15, Q17, Q18, Q22).
- "circularity / 순환성" 문제 지적이 Pressbooks, Moss, MacIntyre 해석에서 반복된다 (Q21).
- "elitism / exclusion / women, slaves, young"이 critical literature에서 반복적으로 phronesis의 사회적 한계로 제기된다 (Q5, Q20).
