## Quotes & evidence

### Q1 — leverage point의 정의 ("작은 변화가 모든 것을 바꾸는 지점")
> "Folks who do systems analysis have a great belief in 'leverage points.' These are places within a complex system (a corporation, an economy, a living body, a city, an ecosystem) where a small shift in one thing can produce big changes in everything."
>
> — "Leverage Points", Sustainability Institute 1999, p.1 (leverage.txt L23–28)

**Context**: 에세이 첫 문단. 레버리지 포인트가 무엇인지 정의하면서 시작. "은빛 총알, 트림탭, 기적의 치료법" 같은 전설적 비유와 연결.

### Q2 — counterintuitive: 사람들은 레버리지를 *거꾸로* 민다 (Forrester 인용)
> "'People know intuitively where leverage points are,' he says. 'Time after time I've done an analysis of a company, and I've figured out a leverage point—in inventory policy, maybe, or in the relationship between sales force and productive force, or in personnel policy. Then I've gone to the company and discovered that there's already a lot of attention to that point. Everyone is trying very hard to push it in the wrong direction!'"
>
> — "Leverage Points", p.1 (leverage.txt L43–56). Jay Forrester를 메도즈가 인용.

**Context**: 에세이 도입부. 메도즈가 MIT에서 Forrester에게 배운 "favorite story"로 소개. 시스템 사고 전체의 반복 경고 — 직관이 레버리지 위치는 맞히지만 *방향*을 틀린다.

### Q3 — "Leverage points are not intuitive" + 거꾸로 쓰면 문제를 악화시킨다
> "Counterintuitive. That's Forrester's word to describe complex systems. Leverage points are not intuitive. Or if they are, we intuitively use them backward, systematically worsening whatever problems we are trying to solve."
>
> — "Leverage Points", p.2 (leverage.txt L74–79)

**Context**: Forrester의 도시 동역학(저소득 주택이 레버리지 포인트라는 반직관적 발견) 사례 직후. counterintuitiveness 명제를 정식화하는 핵심 문장.

### Q4 — 12 leverage points 최종 목록 (효과가 커지는 순서, 12→1)
> "Places to Intervene in a System (in increasing order of effectiveness)
> 12. Constants, parameters, numbers (such as subsidies, taxes, standards)
> 11. The sizes of buffers and other stabilizing stocks, relative to their flows.
> 10. The structure of material stocks and flows (such as transport networks, population age structures)
> 9. The lengths of delays, relative to the rate of system change
> 8. The strength of negative feedback loops, relative to the impacts they are trying to correct against
> 7. The gain around driving positive feedback loops
> 6. The structure of information flows (who does and does not have access to what kinds of information)
> 5. The rules of the system (such as incentives, punishments, constraints)
> 4. The power to add, change, evolve, or self-organize system structure
> 3. The goals of the system
> 2. The mindset or paradigm out of which the system—its goals, structure, rules, delays, parameters—arises
> 1. The power to transcend paradigms"
>
> — "Leverage Points", p.3, the revised final list (leverage.txt L122–172)

**Context**: 즉흥 초안(9개) 이후 시스템 분석가·활동가 피드백을 받아 재정렬한 "revised list". 본 에세이의 골격.

### Q5 — stock과 flow의 정의 (욕조 비유)
> "The 'state of the system' is whatever standing stock is of importance: amount of water behind the dam, amount of harvestable wood in the forest, number of people in the population, amount of money in the bank, whatever. System states are usually physical stocks, but they could be nonmaterial ones as well: self-confidence, degree of trust in public officials, perceived safety of a neighborhood. There are usually inflows that increase the stock and outflows that decrease it."
>
> — "Leverage Points", p.4 (leverage.txt L199–210)

**Context**: 12개 목록을 설명하기 전 "basic diagram"으로 stock/flow를 정의. 비물질 stock(신뢰·자신감)도 포함한다는 점이 명시됨.

### Q6 — flow가 stock에 누적되는 데 시간이 걸린다 + "정책 변화도 효과가 쌓이는 데 시간이 걸린다"
> "It takes time for flows to accumulate in stocks, just as it takes time for water to fill up or drain out of the tub. Policy changes take time to accumulate their effects."
>
> — "Leverage Points", p.4, bathtub 설명 (leverage.txt L193–199)

**Context**: 욕조 수위(stock)가 수도꼭지(flow) 조작에 굼뜨게 반응하는 것을 시스템의 전형으로 제시. delay의 기초.

### Q7 — negative(balancing) feedback loop의 정의: goal + monitor + response
> "Any negative feedback loop needs a goal (the thermostat setting), a monitoring and signaling device to detect excursions from the goal (the thermostat), and a response mechanism (the furnace and/or air conditioner, fans, heat pipes, fuel, etc.)."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #8 항목, p.9 (leverage.txt L448–456)

**Context**: 균형(부정) 피드백 루프를 온도조절기로 정의. 루프 식별의 구성요소(목표·감지·반응)를 절차적으로 제시.

### Q8 — positive(reinforcing) feedback loop의 정의: self-reinforcing, "the more it works, the more it gains power"
> "A negative feedback loop is self-correcting; a positive feedback loop is self-reinforcing. The more it works, the more it gains power to work some more. The more people catch the flu, the more they infect other people. The more babies are born, the more people grow up to have babies. The more money you have in the bank, the more interest you earn, the more money you have in the bank."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #7 항목, p.11 (leverage.txt L559–566)

**Context**: 보강 루프의 정의와 식별 단서("the more X, the more X"). 인플루엔자·출생·이자의 세 예시.

### Q9 — positive loop는 멈추지 않으면 자기 파괴 → 그래서 gain을 줄이는 것이 더 강한 레버리지
> "Positive feedback loops are sources of growth, explosion, erosion, and collapse in systems. A system with an unchecked positive loop ultimately will destroy itself. ... Reducing the gain around a positive loop—slowing the growth—is usually a more powerful leverage point in systems than strengthening negative loops, and much preferable to letting the positive loop run."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #7 항목, p.11 (leverage.txt L529–560)

**Context**: 왜 #7(보강 루프의 gain)이 #8(균형 루프 강화)보다 상위 레버리지인지의 랭킹 논리. "브레이크를 더 좋게 만들기보다 차 속도를 줄여라."

### Q10 — delay의 역할: 피드백 지연이 진동·붕괴를 낳는다
> "A delay in a feedback process is critical relative to rates of change in the system state that the feedback loop is trying to control. Delays that are too short cause overreaction, 'chasing your tail,' oscillations amplified by the jumpiness of the response. Delays that are too long cause damped, sustained, or exploding oscillations, depending on how much too long. At the extreme, they cause chaos."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #9 항목, p.8–9 (leverage.txt L416–433)

**Context**: 4층 욕조/런던 호텔 샤워 일화 직후. 지연이 시스템 행동(진동·붕괴)을 어떻게 결정하는지.

### Q11 — "Overshoots, oscillations, and collapses are always caused by delays."
> "Delays determine how fast systems can react, how accurately they hit their targets, and how timely is the information passed around a system. Overshoots, oscillations, and collapses are always caused by delays."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4 "Ubiquitous Delays", p.105 (primer.txt L429–431)

**Context**: 지연 절에서 단언. 진단 시 "진동·오버슈트·붕괴가 보이면 지연을 찾으라"는 절차로 환원되는 명제.

### Q12 — parameter(12번, 최약 레버리지): "사람들 주의의 99%가 여기 가지만 레버리지는 거의 없다"
> "Parameters are the points of least leverage on my list of interventions. Diddling with the details, arranging the deck chairs on the Titanic. Probably 90—no 95—no 99 percent of our attention goes to parameters, but there's not a lot of leverage in them. ... But they rarely change behavior. If the system is chronically stagnant, parameter changes rarely kick-start it. If it's wildly variable, they don't usually stabilize it. If it's growing out of control, they don't brake it."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #12 항목, p.6 (leverage.txt L293–308)

**Context**: 가장 약한 레버리지(숫자·세금·보조금)에 대부분의 관심이 쏠리는 미스매치를 지적. counterintuitive 경고의 구체화.

### Q13 — rules(5번): "rule을 바꾸면 행동의 범위·자유도가 바뀐다"; rule에 대한 권력이 진짜 권력
> "The rules of the system define its scope, its boundaries, its degrees of freedom. ... As we try to imagine restructured rules like these and what our behavior would be under them, we come to understand the power of rules. They are high leverage points. Power over the rules is real power. ... If you want to understand the deepest malfunctions of systems, pay attention to the rules, and to who has power over them."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #5 항목, p.14 (leverage.txt L658–669, L698–702)

**Context**: 대학의 채점·학위 규칙을 바꿔 상상하게 하는 교육 예시. 규칙 변경이 왜 상위 레버리지인지.

### Q14 — information flow(6번): 누락된 피드백을 *복원*하는 것이 강력하다 (전기계량기 일화)
> "It's not a parameter adjustment, not a strengthening or weakening of an existing loop. It's a new loop, delivering information to a place where it wasn't going before and therefore causing people to behave differently. ... Missing feedback is one of the most common causes of system malfunction. Adding or restoring information can be a powerful intervention, usually much easier and cheaper than rebuilding physical infrastructure."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #6 항목, p.13 (leverage.txt L622–650)

**Context**: 동일한 집인데 전기계량기 위치가 거실/지하실이냐에 따라 전력 소비가 30% 차이 난 일화 직후. 정보 흐름 구조가 왜 stock/flow 재배치보다 상위인지.

### Q15 — self-organization(4번): "자기조직화는 시스템 회복력의 가장 강한 형태"
> "Self-organization means changing any aspect of a system lower on this list: adding completely new physical structures, such as brains or wings or computers; adding new negative or positive loops; making new rules. The ability to self-organize is the strongest form of system resilience. A system that can evolve can survive almost any change, by changing itself."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #4 항목, p.14–15 (leverage.txt L704–720)

**Context**: 진화·기술발전·사회혁명을 systems lingo로 self-organization이라 부름. 왜 규칙(5번)보다 상위인지.

### Q16 — goals(3번): 시스템 전체의 목표는 그 아래 모든 것을 그 목표에 맞게 비튼다
> "The goal of a system is a leverage point superior to the self-organizing ability of a system. For example, if the goal is to bring more and more of the world under the control of one particular central planning system ... then everything further down the list, physical stocks and flows, feedback loops, information flows, even self-organizing behavior, will be twisted to conform to that goal."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #3 항목, p.16 (leverage.txt L786–797)

**Context**: 목표가 자기조직화보다 상위인 이유. "전체 시스템 목표는 사람들이 말하는 것보다 시스템이 *하는 것*에서 추론된다"(L782–787).

### Q17 — paradigm(2번): 패러다임이 시스템의 원천 ("Paradigms are the sources of systems")
> "The shared idea in the minds of society, the great big unstated assumptions—unstated because unnecessary to state; everyone already knows them—constitute that society's paradigm, or deepest set of beliefs about how the world works. ... Paradigms are the sources of systems. From them, from shared social agreements about the nature of reality, come system goals and information flows, feedbacks, stocks, flows and everything else about systems."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #2 항목, p.17–18 (leverage.txt L859–869)

**Context**: 세금법이 어떻게 쓰이든 사회가 공유한 '공정' 관념대로 실제 세금이 수렴한다는 Forrester 격언 직후. 왜 패러다임이 목표보다도 상위인지.

### Q18 — 패러다임은 가장 바꾸기 어렵지만, 개인 안에서는 한순간에 바뀔 수 있다
> "You could say paradigms are harder to change than anything else about a system, and therefore this item should be lowest on the list, not second-to-highest. But there's nothing necessarily physical or expensive or even slow in the process of paradigm change. In a single individual it can happen in a millisecond. All it takes is a click in the mind, a falling of scales from eyes, a new way of seeing. Whole societies are another matter. They resist challenges to their paradigm harder than they resist anything else."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #2 항목, p.18 (leverage.txt L864–876)

**Context**: 랭킹 논리의 핵심 긴장 — '바꾸기 어려움'과 '레버리지 크기'를 분리. 개인 차원 전환은 즉각적이나 사회 차원은 최강의 저항.

### Q19 — transcending paradigms(1번): 어떤 패러다임도 '참'이 아님을 깨닫는 것이 최상위 레버리지
> "There is yet one leverage point that is even higher than changing a paradigm. That is to keep oneself unattached in the arena of paradigms, to stay flexible, to realize that no paradigm is 'true,' that every one, including the one that sweetly shapes your own worldview, is a tremendously limited understanding of an immense and amazing universe that is far beyond human comprehension."
>
> — "Leverage Points", #1 항목, p.19 (leverage.txt L915–924)

**Context**: 최상위 레버리지. 특정 패러다임으로의 전환이 아니라 "패러다임이 있다는 것 자체를 패러다임으로 보는" 메타 위치.

### Q20 — "the higher the leverage point, the more the system will resist changing it"
> "Every item has exceptions that can move it up or down the order of leverage. ... The higher the leverage point, the more the system will resist changing it—that's why societies tend to rub out truly enlightened beings."
>
> — "Leverage Points", "A final caution", p.19 (leverage.txt L919–932)

**Context**: 에세이 결말의 caveat. 랭킹의 핵심 트레이드오프(강력함 ↔ 저항/난이도)를 명시.

### Q21 — "Magical leverage points are not easily accessible, even if we know ... which direction to push"
> "Magical leverage points are not easily accessible, even if we know where they are and which direction to push on them. There are no cheap tickets to mastery. You have to work at it, whether that means rigorously analyzing a system or rigorously casting off your own paradigms ... In the end, it seems that power has less to do with pushing leverage points than it does with strategically, profoundly, madly letting go."
>
> — "Leverage Points", 마지막 문단, p.19 (leverage.txt L933–950)

**Context**: 에세이의 마지막 문장들. 목록이 "sure-fire recipe"가 아님을 다시 못박음.

### Q22 — structure drives behavior: "blaming the individual rarely helps"; 사람을 교체해도 시스템은 안 바뀐다
> "Seeing how individual decisions are rational within the bounds of the information available does not provide an excuse for narrow-minded behavior. It provides an understanding of why that behavior arises. ... Taking out one individual from a position of bounded rationality and putting in another person is not likely to make much difference. Blaming the individual rarely helps create a more desirable outcome."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4 "Bounded Rationality", p.108 (primer.txt L570–577)

**Context**: 학생을 어부·관료·상류층 역할에 넣는 게임으로 bounded rationality를 가르친 직후. "구조가 행동을 낳는다"의 실천적 귀결 — 개인 탓하기 대신 구조 재설계.

### Q23 — bounded rationality의 정의
> "Bounded rationality means that people make quite reasonable decisions based on the information they have. But they don't have perfect information, especially about more distant parts of the system. Fishermen don't know how many fish there are, much less how many fish will be caught by other fishermen that same day."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4, p.106 (primer.txt L485–489). Herbert Simon 개념을 인용·정의.

**Context**: "보이지 않는 발(invisible foot)"·Herbert Simon의 satisficer 개념을 끌어와, 합리적 개인행동이 아무도 원치 않는 집합결과를 낳는 이유로 제시.

### Q24 — 시스템 재설계가 답: 정보·인센티브·목표·제약을 바꿔라
> "The bounded rationality of each actor in a system—determined by the information, incentives, disincentives, goals, stresses, and constraints impinging on that actor—may or may not lead to decisions that further the welfare of the system as a whole. If they do not, putting new actors into the same system will not improve the system's performance. What makes a difference is redesigning the system to improve the information, incentives, disincentives, goals, stresses, and constraints that have an effect on specific actors."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4, p.110, 본문 측면 강조 박스 (primer.txt L635–644)

**Context**: bounded rationality 절의 결론. 진단이 끝난 뒤 어디를 손대야 하는지를 명시 (레버리지 포인트 5·6·3과 연결되는 항목들).

### Q25 — boundary는 시스템의 진짜 경계가 아니라 우리가 만든 것; 목적에 따라 정해진다
> "The lesson of boundaries is hard even for systems thinkers to get. There is no single, legitimate boundary to draw around a system. We have to invent boundaries for clarity and sanity; and boundaries can produce problems when we forget that we've artificially created them. ... There are no separate systems. The world is a continuum. Where to draw a boundary around a system depends on the purpose of the discussion—the questions we want to ask."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4 "Nonexistent Boundaries", p.97 (primer.txt L113–121)

**Context**: "구름(clouds)" — 다이어그램에서 무시된 stock/sink — 이 시스템 surprise의 원천이라는 논의. 경계 설정이 자의적임을 메도즈 본인이 인정하는 대목.

### Q26 — 경계를 너무 넓게 잡는 함정: "내 모델이 네 모델보다 크다" 게임
> "Systems analysts often fall into the opposite trap: making boundaries too large. They have a habit of producing diagrams that cover several pages with small print and many arrows connecting everything with everything. There is the system! they say. ... This 'my model is bigger than your model' game results in enormously complicated analyses, which produce piles of information that may only serve to obscure the answers to the questions at hand."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4, p.98 (primer.txt L142–152)

**Context**: 경계를 너무 좁게 잡는 함정(고속도로→주거지 유입) 직후. 시스템 사고가 과잉 복잡화로 무너지는 지점을 저자 본인이 지목 (analysis paralysis의 1차 자기진단).

### Q27 — limiting factor: "가장 부족한 입력이 가장 중요하다" (Liebig의 최소량 법칙)
> "It doesn't matter how much nitrogen is available to the grain, he said, if what's short is phosphorus. It does no good to pour on more phosphorus, if the problem is low potassium. ... At any given time, the input that is most important to a system is the one that is most limiting."
>
> — *Thinking in Systems* (2008), Ch.4 "Layers of Limits", p.101 (primer.txt L266–280)

**Context**: 산업 생산·곡물의 다중 입력 목록 직후. 어디에 개입할지(limiting factor)를 식별하는 진단 규칙. "성장 자체가 무엇이 limiting인지를 바꾼다"는 동적 통찰로 이어짐(L313–321).

## Critiques & limits (raw, 평가 금지)

### C1 — 메도즈 본인: 목록은 엄밀히 논증된 게 아니라 "work in progress", 순서가 미끄럽다
> "As with most of the stuff that comes to me in boil-over mode, this list was not exactly tightly reasoned. ... So, what you are about to read is a work in progress. It's not a simple, sure-fire recipe for finding leverage points. Rather, it's an invitation to think more broadly about the many ways there might be to get systems to change."
>
> 그리고: "So much has to be said to qualify this list. It is tentative and its order is slithery. Every item has exceptions that can move it up or down the order of leverage."
>
> — "Leverage Points", p.3 및 p.19 (leverage.txt L129–166, L920–924). 저자 본인 caveat.

**Target**: 12개 랭킹을 고정된 절차/공식으로 쓰려는 시도.
**Context**: 에세이의 도입과 결말 양쪽에서 반복. 후속 스킬이 "순위를 결정론으로 쓰지 말라"는 경계 조항을 짤 1차 근거.

### C2 — 메도즈 본인: 시스템은 통제·예측·최적화가 불가능하다
> "Self-organizing, nonlinear, feedback systems are inherently unpredictable. They are not controllable. ... For any objective other than the most trivial, we can't optimize; we don't even know what to optimize. We can't impose our will upon a system. We can listen to what the system tells us ... We can't control systems or figure them out. But we can dance with them!"
>
> — "Dancing With Systems" (2001), donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/

**Target**: 시스템 사고를 "정답 레버리지를 찾아 밀면 통제된다"는 식으로 쓰는 것.
**Context**: 유고 발췌. 시스템 사고의 산출물이 '통제'가 아니라 '함께 추는 춤'임을 명시 — 정량모델 과신·통제 환상에 대한 저자 본인의 한계 설정.

### C3 — 메도즈 본인의 실천 가이드(한계 내장): 손대기 전에 먼저 관찰하라 / 모델을 공개해 반박받아라 / 모를 땐 배워라
> "GET THE BEAT — Before you disturb the system in any way, watch how it behaves."
> "EXPOSE YOUR MENTAL MODELS TO THE OPEN AIR — Get your model out there where it can be shot at. Invite others to challenge your assumptions."
> "STAY HUMBLE. STAY A LEARNER — The thing to do, when you don't know, is not to bluff and not to freeze, but to learn."
>
> — "Dancing With Systems" (2001), "The Dance" 가이드라인 (donellameadows.org/dancing-with-systems/)

**Target**: 빠른 1턴 진단이 관찰·검증 없이 개입 처방으로 직행하는 것.
**Context**: 시스템 사고의 절차에 내장된 자기제약. 분석을 멈추고 행동으로 옮길 때조차 "먼저 beat를 들어라"는 선행 조건.

### C4 — 학술 비판 (Abson et al. 2017): 메도즈 프레임은 어디에 개입할지를 다루지만 *어떻게 식별/실행하는지*는 공백
> "with few notable exceptions ..., one of the most important facets of systems thinking has been largely overlooked to date: Where in a system should we intervene to change its overall behaviour?"
> 그리고: "Here we outline our interpretation of key steps to refine and operationalize Meadows' vision into a concrete research agenda" / "An understanding of such potential interactions between deep and shallow leverage points represents a crucial gap in our current understanding."
>
> — Abson, Fischer, Leventon et al., "Leverage points for sustainability transformation", *Ambio* 46:30–39 (2017), pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5226895/

**Target**: 12개 목록이 *실행 가능한 식별 절차*를 제공한다는 가정.
**Context**: 메도즈를 계승하는 학자들조차 "operationalize"가 필요하다고 명시. deep/shallow 레버리지 간 상호작용 이해가 공백이라고 지적.

### C5 — 종합 비판: deep leverage point 과잉강조 → 실행 막막함; 결정론적; 맥락·권력 무시
> "Critics say that focusing too much on these deep changes can cause people and organizations to feel stuck or frustrated because they can't find practical ways to start making a difference."
> 그리고: "Critics say this view makes things too simple and doesn't consider the complexity of social-ecological systems. They argue that it ignores important factors like human choices, power struggles, and unexpected events." / "leverage points can change based on the specific system and its special traits ... how power dynamics affect how easy or difficult it is to make changes at different leverage points."
>
> — Systems Thinking Alliance, "Leverage Points in System Transformation: Insights & Critiques" (systemsthinkingalliance.org/...)

**Target**: 상위(패러다임·목표) 레버리지가 항상 우월하다는 함의; 랭킹의 보편성.
**Context**: 2차 종합 글이라 개별 비판자 출처는 미명시(아래 Gaps 참조). deep point가 매력적이지만 실행 진입점이 불분명하다는 실무 비판을 정리.

### C6 — 정량 시스템 다이내믹스 모델 비판: 역사 데이터 재현 실패, 반증 어려움 (Solow 1972 등)
> 검색 종합: "Economists like Robert Solow contended in 1972 that such models fail to adequately replicate historical data, thereby undermining confidence in their explanatory power." / "system dynamics models often prioritize internal structural consistency over empirical falsifiability, making it difficult to rigorously test and refute hypotheses ... a model can match historical data for the wrong structural reasons."
>
> — "A Critical Review of the Criticisms of System Dynamics" 및 System dynamics 종합 (researchgate.net/publication/271827939...; grokipedia.com/page/System_dynamics)

**Target**: 시스템을 정량 모델로 돌리면 레버리지를 신뢰성 있게 찾을 수 있다는 주장(메도즈가 "give us a few months of computer modeling and we'll figure it out"이라 한 부분, leverage.txt L88–95).
**Context**: Solow 비판의 1차 원문은 미확보(2차 요약) — Gaps에 표기. 정량 모델의 반증 가능성·집계 가정(homogeneity)의 한계가 핵심.

## Recurring observations (raw, 해석 금지)

- "in the wrong direction" / "backward" — 직관이 레버리지의 *방향*을 거꾸로 민다는 표현이 도입부와 #8(가격), #9(금융지연), #12 전반에 반복 (Q2, Q3, leverage.txt L56, L74–78, L428–429, L510–511).
- "the more X, the more X" 형태 — 보강 루프 식별의 반복 문형 (Q8: 독감/출생/이자/토양침식/핵분열, leverage.txt L561–567, L520–528).
- "goal / monitor / response" 3요소 — 균형 루프(온도조절기)를 항상 이 세 부품으로 분해 (Q7).
- "takes time" / "delay" / "accumulate" — flow→stock 누적 지연이 욕조·정책·전력·인구에서 반복 (Q6, Q10, Q11, primer.txt delays 절 전체).
- "structure" vs "players/individuals" 대비 — 사람 교체 ≠ 시스템 변화; 구조 재설계가 답 (Q22, Q24).
- "boundaries ... of our own making" / "depends on the purpose" — 경계는 자의적·목적 의존이라는 표현이 boundary 절 전체에 반복 (Q25, Q26).
- "work in progress" / "tentative" / "slithery" / "exceptions" / "caveat" — 목록의 잠정성·예외성을 저자가 반복 표기 (C1, leverage.txt L129–166, L920–924).
- "the higher ... the more the system will resist" — 레버리지 크기와 변경 난이도의 역상관이 패러다임(Q18)과 final caution(Q20)에서 반복.
- "we systems folks / systems-heads / systems analysts" — 자기지칭으로 직관 대비 학습된 반직관을 강조하는 표현 반복 (leverage.txt L80, L405, L611).

## Gaps / open questions

- **한국어 1차/수용 자료 부재**: "도넬라 메도즈", "시스템 사고", "레버리지 포인트", "성장의 한계" 등으로 검색하면 국내 번역서(『ESG와 세상을 읽는 시스템 법칙』/『시스템으로 사고하기』 류) 발췌·블로그 요약은 있으나, 메도즈 발언을 페이지/타임스탬프와 함께 원문 대조 가능한 한국어 1차 출처를 확보하지 못함. 본 raw는 영어 원전 중심으로만 작성. 국내 번역서의 정확한 쪽수·역어 대조가 필요하면 번역본 실물 확인 추천.
- **MECE/맥킨지·Munger latticework·5 Whys와의 명시적 대비**: 메도즈 *본인*이 이 셋을 직접 호명해 "내 방식은 여기서 다르다"고 말한 1차 발언은 못 찾음. 대신 간접 근거가 본문에 있음 — (a) 5 Whys류 단일 인과: "our minds like to think about single causes neatly producing single effects ... But we live in a world in which many causes routinely come together to produce many effects"(primer.txt L221–226, Q27 직전); (b) 정적 MECE 분해 대비: 경계가 목적 의존·동적이며 stock/flow는 시간에 따라 누적된다는 점(Q5, Q6, Q25, Q26); (c) Munger latticework(수평적 모델 겹침) 대비: 메도즈는 모델을 *겹치는* 게 아니라 stock·flow·loop·delay·leverage라는 *수직 구조/동역학*으로 본다는 점이 12점 랭킹(Q4, Q20)에 내장. 이들은 대비의 재료일 뿐 메도즈의 직접 비교 발언은 아님 — 후속 단계가 경계 조항을 쓸 때 "직접 인용 아님"으로 다뤄야 함.
- **Solow 1972 비판 1차 원문 미확보**: C6은 2차 요약에 의존. 정확한 인용이 필요하면 Solow의 *World Dynamics* 서평(원전) 추적 필요.
- **C5의 개별 비판자 미명시**: Systems Thinking Alliance 글이 비판을 "critics say"로 묶어 개별 학자/논문을 명시하지 않음. 동료심사 출처로 보강하려면 Abson et al.(C4)과 그 인용문헌(Fischer & Riechers 2019, "A leverage points perspective on sustainability")을 추가 추적 추천.
- **인용 개수**: 1차 인용 27개(Q1–Q27), 비판 6개(C1–C6)로 최소 기준(5개) 충족.
