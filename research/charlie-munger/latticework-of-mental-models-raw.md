## Quotes & evidence

### Q1 — 격자틀(latticework) framing 자체
> "What is elementary, worldly wisdom? Well, the first rule is that you can't really know anything if you just remember isolated facts and try and bang 'em back. If the facts don't hang together on a latticework of theory, you don't have them in a usable form.
>
> You've got to have models in your head. And you've got to array your experience—both vicarious and direct—on this latticework of models. You may have noticed students who just try to remember and pound back what is remembered. Well, they fail in school and in life. You've got to hang experience on a latticework of models in your head."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994 ("Elementary Worldly Wisdom"), 도입부 "What is elementary, worldly wisdom?" 단락 (transcript PDF p.1)

**Context**: 강연 초반, "worldly wisdom"의 첫 번째 규칙을 정의하는 대목. stock picking을 가르치기 전에 "general education / 격자틀"이 먼저라는 Grandma's rule 비유 직후.

### Q2 — 복수 모델 강제 + man-with-a-hammer (단일학문 왜곡)
> "What are the models? Well, the first rule is that you've got to have multiple models—because if you just have one or two that you're using, the nature of human psychology is such that you'll torture reality so that it fits your models, or at least you'll think it does. You become the equivalent of a chiropractor who, of course, is the great boob in medicine.
>
> It's like the old saying, 'To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail.' And of course, that's the way the chiropractor goes about practicing medicine. But that's a perfectly disastrous way to think and a perfectly disastrous way to operate in the world. So you've got to have multiple models."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, "What are the models?" 단락 (transcript PDF p.1–2)

**Context**: 격자틀을 정의한 직후, 왜 모델이 *여러 개*여야 하는지의 근거. man-with-a-hammer는 단일 모델 의존이 일으키는 인지 왜곡(현실을 모델에 맞춰 비트는 것)의 이름.

### Q3 — 모델은 여러 학문에서 와야 한다 + 단일학문 학과 비판
> "And the models have to come from multiple disciplines—because all the wisdom of the world is not to be found in one little academic department. That's why poetry professors, by and large, are so unwise in a worldly sense. They don't have enough models in their heads. So you've got to have models across a fair array of disciplines."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, Q2 바로 다음 단락 (transcript PDF p.2)

**Context**: 복수 모델 요구를 학문 경계로 확장. "한 작은 학과 안에 세상의 모든 지혜가 있지 않다"가 academic silo 비판의 핵심 문장.

### Q4 — "the big ideas from the big disciplines" / 80~90개 모델 주장
> "You may say, 'My God, this is already getting way too tough.' But, fortunately, it isn't that tough—because 80 or 90 important models will carry about 90% of the freight in making you a worldly-wise person. And, of those, only a mere handful really carry very heavy freight."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, Q3 다음 단락 (transcript PDF p.2)

**Context**: 모델을 *모두* 익힐 필요는 없고, 소수의 load-bearing 모델만으로 90%를 감당한다는 주장. "only a mere handful really carry very heavy freight"가 big-ideas-from-big-disciplines의 압축.

### Q5 — 어떤 학문의 어떤 모델인가 (수학·복리·순열조합·회계)
> "First there's mathematics. Obviously, you've got to be able to handle numbers and quantities—basic arithmetic. And the great useful model, after compound interest, is the elementary math of permutations and combinations. ...
>
> Obviously, you have to know accounting. It's the language of practical business life. ... But you have to know enough about it to understand its limitations—because although accounting is the starting place, it's only a crude approximation."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, "So let's briefly review what kind of models..." 이후 단락들 (transcript PDF p.2–3)

**Context**: 격자틀을 구성하는 구체 모델의 첫 묶음. 복리(compound interest)를 가장 먼저, 그다음 순열·조합. 회계는 "필요하되 그 한계까지 알아야" 한다(모델을 알되 적용 경계를 안다).

### Q6 — 가장 신뢰할 만한 모델은 hard science / engineering, 그리고 physics·biology·psychology
> "Which models are the most reliable? Well, obviously, the models that come from hard science and engineering are the most reliable models on this Earth. ...
>
> And, of course, the engineering idea of a backup system is a very powerful idea. The engineering idea of breakpoints—that's a very powerful model, too. The notion of a critical mass—that comes out of physics—is a very powerful model. ...
>
> I suppose the next most reliable models are from biology/physiology because, after all, all of us are programmed by our genetic makeup to be much the same. And then when you get into psychology, of course, it gets very much more complicated. But it's an ungodly important subject if you're going to have any worldly wisdom."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, "Which models are the most reliable?" 단락 + 이후 (transcript PDF p.4–5)

**Context**: 학문별 신뢰도 위계를 명시. engineering의 backup system·breakpoint, physics의 critical mass를 "very powerful model"로 호명. 신뢰도 순서: hard science/engineering > biology/physiology > psychology(가장 복잡하지만 가장 중요).

### Q7 — 두 모델/힘이 결합할 때 (lollapalooza의 USC판 — 단어는 없음)
> "Now you get into the cognitive function as distinguished from the perceptual function. And there, you are equally—more than equally in fact—likely to be misled. ... So when circumstances combine in certain ways—or more commonly, your fellow man starts acting like the magician and manipulates you on purpose by causing your cognitive dysfunction—you're a patsy.
>
> And so just as a man working with a tool has to know its limitations, a man working with his cognitive apparatus has to know its limitations."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, 지각·인지 왜곡 단락 (transcript PDF p.5)

**Context**: USC 강연에는 "lollapalooza"라는 단어가 등장하지 않으나, "circumstances combine in certain ways"로 복수 힘의 결합 개념이 이미 제시됨. man-with-a-tool(=hammer)의 한계 인식이 다시 반복.

### Q8 — lollapalooza tendency 정의 (Harvard 1995, 원전)
> "The Tendency to Get Extreme Consequences from Confluences of Psychological Tendencies Acting in Favor of a Particular Outcome ... What happens when these standard psychological tendencies combine? ... The clear answer is the combination greatly increases power to change behavior, compared to the power of merely one tendency acting alone. ... When you get these lollapalooza effects, you will almost always find four or five of these things working together."
>
> — "The Psychology of Human Misjudgment", Harvard, 1995년 6월 1일 (transcript, "Lollapalooza Tendency" 항목; fs.blog / jamesclear.com 전문)

**Context**: 25개(원래 강연 시점엔 ~20개) 심리경향을 모두 나열한 뒤 마지막에 두는 결론 항목. lollapalooza = 여러 경향이 *같은 방향으로 동시에* 작동해 비선형적 극단 결과를 내는 현상. 탐지 신호로 "보통 4~5개가 함께 작동"을 제시.

### Q9 — two-track analysis (문제를 실제로 돌리는 절차)
> "Personally, I've gotten so that I now use a kind of two-track analysis. First, what are the factors that really govern the interests involved, rationally considered? And second, what are the subconscious influences where the brain at a subconscious level is automatically doing these things—which by and large are useful, but which often misfunction.
>
> One approach is rationality—the way you'd work out a bridge problem: by evaluating the real interests, the real probabilities and so forth. And the other is to evaluate the psychological factors that cause subconscious conclusions—many of which are wrong."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, Pascal 인용 직후 단락 (transcript PDF p.6); 동일 발췌가 *Poor Charlie's Almanack* "Charlie's checklists / two-track analysis"로도 재수록

**Context**: 격자틀을 *적용하는* 운영 방식. Track 1 = 합리적 이해관계·확률(브리지 문제처럼), Track 2 = 잠재의식적 심리편향 점검. 문제 하나를 두 트랙으로 동시에 돌린다.

### Q10 — circle of competence (격자틀의 경계 조건)
> "Again, that is a very, very powerful idea. Every person is going to have a circle of competence. And it's going to be very hard to advance that circle. ...
>
> So you have to figure out what your own aptitudes are. If you play games where other people have the aptitudes and you don't, you're going to lose. ... You have to figure out where you've got an edge. And you've got to play within your own circle of competence."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, Berkshire가 high-tech를 피하는 이유 설명 직후 (transcript PDF p.16)

**Context**: Munger가 자신과 Buffett이 high-tech를 "personal inadequacies" 때문에 피한다고 말한 직후. 격자틀로 세상을 분석하더라도, 자기 강점의 경계(circle of competence) 안에서만 베팅한다는 bounding 조건.

### Q11 — man-with-a-hammer 재등장 (효율적 시장 이론 사례)
> "Again, to the man with a hammer, every problem looks like a nail. If you're good at manipulating higher mathematics in a consistent way, why not make an assumption which enables you to use your tool?"
>
> — USC Business School, 1994, 효율적 시장 이론(efficient market theory)을 "bonkers"라 부른 직후 (transcript PDF p.18)

**Context**: 같은 강연 안에서 man-with-a-hammer가 두 번째로 등장. 이번엔 수학에 능한 경제학자들이 그 도구를 쓰려고 비현실적 가정을 한다는 구체 사례에 적용. 단일 도구 편향이 학계 전문가에게도 적용됨을 보임.

### Q12 — 한국 수용: "망치 든 사람" 편향과 다학제 해법
> "망치 든 사람에게는 모든 문제가 못으로 보인다." … "여러 학문에 걸쳐 다양한 역량을 갖추면 다양한 도구를 보유하게 되므로, 망치 든 사람 편향으로 인한 인식 오류를 제한할 수 있을 것"
>
> — 김재현, "찰리 멍거 다시 읽기②", 머니투데이, 2025-05-31

## Critiques & limits (raw, 평가 금지)

### C1 — 격자틀은 명시지로 환원되지 않는다(암묵지 문제), 모델 나열만으로 실력이 안 는다
> "the mental models that make Buffett and Munger so effective are divided into two types" — explicit and tacit ... "the mental models that I believe best explains a practitioner's success" are tacit and "nearly impossible to communicate." ... "you should be suspicious of non-practitioners writing about the mental models of practitioners. Without actual experience running a business, [the] writing works at the wrong resolution."
>
> — Cedric Chin, "The Mental Model Fallacy", Commoncog, 2018-11-13

**Target**: "80~90개의 모델을 머릿속 격자틀에 배열하면 worldly-wise해진다"(Q1, Q4)는 주장 — 즉 모델을 *알면* 실력이 따라온다는 전제.
**Context**: Munger/Buffett을 다루는 "mental models" 담론(특히 모델 목록을 나열하는 블로그류)을 겨냥. 핵심 논증은 Munger를 효과적으로 만드는 모델 다수가 암묵지(tacit)라 목록·강연으로 전수 불가하다는 것.
**Translation (ko)**: Buffett·Munger를 효과적으로 만드는 멘탈 모델은 명시지/암묵지 둘로 나뉘며, 성공을 가장 잘 설명하는 모델은 암묵지여서 "거의 전달 불가능"하다. 실무 경험 없는 사람이 실무자의 멘탈 모델을 쓰면 "해상도가 틀린" 글이 된다.

### C2 — Munger 본인이 인정한 한계: "기수(chauffeur) 지식" — 격자틀이 피상적 박학으로 흐를 위험
> "In this world we have two kinds of knowledge. One is Planck knowledge, the people who really know. They've paid the dues, they have the aptitude. And then we've got chauffeur knowledge. They've learned the talk. ... And you are going to have the problem in your life of getting the responsibility into the people with the Planck knowledge and away from the people with the chauffeur knowledge."
>
> — Charles T. Munger, 2007 USC Law School Commencement (fs.blog/two-types-of-knowledge)

**Target**: 여러 학문의 "큰 아이디어"를 두루 익히라는 격자틀 처방(Q3, Q4)이 자칫 "말만 배운" 피상적 박학(dabbling)으로 변질될 위험 — Munger 본인이 경계로 제시.
**Context**: Max Planck의 운전기사가 강연을 통째로 외워 흉내 냈다는 일화. 진짜 dues를 치른 지식(Planck)과 화술만 익힌 지식(chauffeur)의 구별. Munger 스스로 격자틀의 부작용(겉만 아는 것)을 인정하는 경계 조항으로 읽힘.
**Translation (ko)**: 세상엔 두 종류의 지식이 있다 — 진짜 아는 Planck 지식(대가를 치르고 적성을 갖춘 사람)과, 화술만 익힌 chauffeur 지식. 인생의 과제는 책임을 Planck 지식 가진 이에게 맡기고 chauffeur 지식 가진 이에게서 떼어내는 것.

### C3 — Munger 본인이 인정한 경계: 자신의 격자틀이 안 통하는 도메인(circle of competence 밖)
> "And Warren and I don't feel like we have any great advantage in the high-tech sector. In fact, we feel like we're at a big disadvantage in trying to understand the nature of technical developments in software, computer chips or what have you. So we tend to avoid that stuff, based on our personal inadequacies."
>
> — USC Business School, 1994 (transcript PDF p.16)

**Target**: 다학제 격자틀로 "어떤 문제든 분석 가능"하다는 (암묵적) 보편 적용성 — Munger 스스로 특정 도메인에선 격자틀 보유에도 불구하고 우위가 없다고 인정.
**Context**: circle of competence(Q10) 논의의 근거 사례. 자신과 Buffett이 high-tech를 피하는 이유는 "personal inadequacies", 즉 격자틀을 가져도 deep 전문성이 없는 영역이 존재함을 인정.
**Translation (ko)**: 나와 워런은 하이테크 분야에 별 우위가 없다고 느낀다. 오히려 소프트웨어·반도체 같은 기술 변화의 본질을 이해하는 데 큰 열세라고 느낀다. 그래서 우리의 부족함을 근거로 그런 것들을 피한다.

### C4 — 전문성 연구: 저효율(low-validity) 환경 밖에서는 도메인 특화 전문가가 우위
> "Experts can outperform novices significantly due to their ability to draw on a wealth of stored knowledge relevant to their domain. However, generalists excel in low-validity or complex environments where patterns are difficult to discern or do not exist." ... domain-specific expertise "can also create rigidity, making it harder for individuals to adapt their skills to new or different contexts."
>
> — (전문성/일반가 비교 정리, cognitive psychology 자료 종합; 검색 결과 요약 — 단일 1차 논문 미확정)

**Target**: 다학제 일반가(generalist)가 단일 학문 깊이파기보다 우월하다는 함의 — 패턴이 뚜렷한(high-validity) 도메인에선 도메인 특화 전문가가 우위라는 반대 증거.
**Context**: 전문성 연구에서 일반가의 우위는 "저효율·패턴 식별 곤란" 환경에 한정되며, 패턴이 분명한 도메인에선 deep 전문가가 우위. 적용 도메인 미스매치의 자료.
**Translation (ko)**: 전문가는 도메인 관련 축적 지식을 끌어 쓸 수 있어 초보를 크게 능가한다. 다만 일반가는 패턴 식별이 어렵거나 패턴이 없는 저효율·복잡 환경에서 빛난다. 도메인 특화 전문성은 경직성을 낳아 새 맥락 적응을 어렵게 만들 수도 있다.

### C5 — 비실무 해설자가 모델을 "얕은 복사본"으로 앵무새질하는 것에 대한 경계
> "those who merely parrot mental models as shallow copies should be viewed with suspicion ... Knowing something in theory and knowing something in practice aren't the same thing."
>
> — Cedric Chin, "The Mental Model Fallacy", Commoncog, 2018-11-13 (검색 요약 인용)

**Target**: 격자틀을 "모델 목록 암기"로 환원하는 통속적 수용 — 이론적 앎과 실무적 앎의 간극.
**Context**: C1과 동일 논문. 모델을 얕게 복사해 읊는 사람을 의심하라는 처방. (C1과 출처 동일, 별도 각도라 분리.)
**Translation (ko)**: 멘탈 모델을 얕은 복사본으로 그저 앵무새처럼 읊는 사람은 의심해야 한다. 이론으로 아는 것과 실무로 아는 것은 같지 않다.
