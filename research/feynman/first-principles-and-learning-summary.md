# Decomposition — Richard Feynman: First-Principles Thinking & Learning Method

- **Source**: [first-principles-and-learning-raw.md](./first-principles-and-learning-raw.md)
- **Date**: 2026-05-23

## One-line essence
Refuse the verbal label, derive a checkable consequence from the underlying mechanism, and trust the result over any authority — including your own fluent explanation.

> 두 결(first-principles · learning method)은 Feynman 본인 안에서 **같은 한 동작**으로 작동한다: "이름을 가진 무언가"를 "기계적 동작"으로 분해해 현실에 대고 시험하는 것. First-principles는 *새로운 문제*에 이 동작을 적용한 것이고, Feynman technique은 *이미 안다고 믿는 것*에 같은 동작을 적용한 것이다. 결정적으로 공유되는 토대는 Q2의 "you must not fool yourself" — 두 결 모두 이 자기-기만 방지 장치를 깔고 작동한다 (근거: Q2, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q11, Q15).

## Core principles

1. **Not-fooling-yourself is the prerequisite, not the goal.** Feynman explicitly calls "you are the easiest person to fool" *the* first principle — before any reasoning move, you install the posture that surfaces your own counter-evidence. Without this floor every other move (simplification, derivation, explanation) becomes cargo-cult — form perfect, mechanism absent (근거: Q2, Q3, Q4).

2. **"Knowing the name of X" ≠ "knowing X."** The operating distinction Feynman traces to his father and repeats across 15 years (1966 NSTA → 1981 BBC): names, jargon, formulas, citations are *facts about people* — what they call the thing. The thing itself is what does the work. Every Feynman-style probe is engineered to force the conversation off the label onto the mechanism (근거: Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9).

3. **The three-step procedure: guess → derive a consequence → compare to reality.** Same shape at three scales: a child's hypothesis about a bird (Q7), the physicist's hypothesis about a new law (Q11), the student's hypothesis about Brewster's angle being polarization (Q9). The non-negotiable step is the third — comparison to a concrete instance you cannot talk your way out of (근거: Q7, Q9, Q11).

4. **Explanation requires an explicit frame.** "Why" has no natural floor; every causal answer presupposes a layer that is itself a candidate for further why-questions (Q14). So Feynman insists on naming what you are *permitted to assume* and what you are not. First-principles thinking is not infinite regress — it is making the frame explicit so the regress can stop honestly (근거: Q12, Q14).

5. **Reduce relentlessly, but never falsify by reducing.** Feynman opens *Lectures* by reducing all of physics to one sentence about atoms (Q1) — and in the same career refuses to "explain" magnets in rubber-band analogies because the analogy would be a lie (Q12, Q13). Simplification is for compression, not for fabricating familiarity that is not there (근거: Q1, Q12, Q13).

6. **Authority is a question about provenance, not a stopping point.** "Science is the belief in the ignorance of experts." When someone says "science has shown," replace it with "this experiment has shown" and trace the chain back to the actual observation. You have as much right as anyone to judge — provided you do the listening (근거: Q17, Q18).

## The mental moves (재현 가능한 절차)

The moves below apply equally to *learning* (auditing what you already think you know) and to *first-principles work* (attacking a fresh problem). Where the two strands diverge in execution I mark it inline.

1. **Install the floor: "I am the easiest person to fool."** Before starting, name the specific way your current understanding could be fluent-but-empty. Without this step, every later move just generates better-sounding cargo (근거: Q2, Q3).

2. **Strip the names.** Take the thing you want to understand (a concept you're studying, or a problem you're attacking) and replace every term-of-art with what it *does*. "Brewster's angle" becomes "the reflection-angle where light comes off the water polarized." "Index of refraction" becomes "the thing that makes a stick look bent in water" (근거: Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9).

3. **Open a "things I don't know" surface.** Feynman's prototypical artifact (Q15): a fresh notebook titled with the gap, not the answer. The act of writing the title *organizes the question*, not the knowledge. For *learning*: list the subjects/branches whose kernels you cannot derive. For *first-principles*: list the assumptions inside the problem you cannot currently justify (근거: Q15).

4. **Reorganize from kernels — disassemble, look for raw edges, reassemble.** Gleick's description of Feynman at the notebook: "disassembling each branch of physics, oiling the parts, putting them back together, looking all the while for the raw edges and inconsistencies." The work is finding the *essential kernel* of each subject — the minimal claim from which the rest can be re-derived. Q1 (atoms in one sentence) is what a kernel looks like (근거: Q1, Q15).

5. **Make the frame of explanation explicit.** Before answering "why," declare what level of mechanism you are willing to treat as given. "Magnets attract" assumes Maxwell; "Maxwell's equations hold" assumes empirical electromagnetism; etc. Stating the frame stops both infinite regress and false reduction (근거: Q12, Q14).

6. **Guess, derive a consequence, look.** Form a candidate mechanism. From it, derive *one prediction* about a concrete case you can check — preferably one you have not yet looked at. Then *actually look*. If the prediction fails, the mechanism is wrong; smartness and beauty are irrelevant (근거: Q7, Q9, Q11).

7. **Apply the explanation to a thing in front of you.** The Brazil-bay diagnostic (Q9): when a student claims to understand Brewster's law, point at the sea outside the window. Anyone who has learned only the words cannot make the bridge. For self-audit: every claim you "understand" must connect to one *plain, present-tense, non-textbook* instance. If it cannot, you have the name, not the thing (근거: Q9).

8. **Try to derive a freshman-level lecture from it.** Goodstein asked Feynman to explain why spin-½ particles obey Fermi-Dirac to freshmen; Feynman tried and came back: "I couldn't reduce it to the freshman level. That means *we* don't really understand it" (Q16). The move is diagnostic — failure to reduce reveals a gap in the *field's* (or your) mechanism. Crucial nuance: the verdict is "we don't understand," not "you don't study hard enough" (근거: Q16, C2).

9. **Refuse to cheat with a false analogy.** When the simplification would require asserting a mechanism you don't have (magnets ≈ rubber bands), stop. Name the limit ("I can't explain that attraction in terms of anything more familiar to you, because I don't understand it in terms of anything more familiar") rather than ship a fluent lie (근거: Q12, Q13, C5).

10. **Bend over backwards to disclose what could make you wrong.** Active scientific integrity: list the details that could throw doubt on your interpretation, that could falsify your guess, that the audience would need to know to evaluate you fairly. This is what converts a Feynman-style explanation from "I sounded convincing" to "I survived the worst version of my own audit" (근거: Q4).

11. **When stuck, restart from the kernel.** First-principles and learning meet here: the *same* habit of going back to the minimal claim and re-deriving forward — whether the problem is "I can't solve this" (new problem) or "I thought I knew this and don't" (old material). The notebook of things I don't know and the "piddle around with a concrete problem yourself" advice (Q21) are the same move in two postures (근거: Q15, Q21).

## Heuristics & decision rules

- **If your explanation only works in jargon, you have a name, not a thing.** Force a re-statement in plain mechanism; if you can't, that's the gap (근거: Q5, Q6, Q8).
- **If your understanding cannot predict a concrete case in front of you, it's cargo.** Point at the bay (근거: Q3, Q9).
- **If you can't reduce it to a freshman lecture, the field (or you) doesn't yet understand it.** Note: the diagnostic is honest naming of a community gap, not personal shaming (근거: Q16, C2).
- **If the simplification requires a false familiar analogy, refuse the simplification.** Better to say "I can't explain this in familiar terms" than to install a wrong model in the listener's head (근거: Q12, Q13).
- **If a "why" chain has no declared stopping frame, you're regressing infinitely.** Declare what is assumed-given before answering (근거: Q12, Q14).
- **If a guess disagrees with experiment, it's wrong — full stop, regardless of authority, beauty, or your own attachment.** "If it disagrees with experiment, it's wrong. That's all there is to it" (근거: Q11).
- **If "science has shown X" is the form, ask "which experiment, which observation, where?"** Replace authority-claims with provenance-claims (근거: Q17).
- **If your audit produced a smooth, satisfying simplification, suspect it.** Smoothness can manufacture illusion of explanatory depth — test against a case you haven't seen (근거: C6).

## Anti-patterns (이 인물이 거부하는 것)

- **Memorization that doesn't bridge to a concrete instance.** The Brazil students could recite Brewster's law but couldn't see polarized light through the window (근거: Q8, Q9, Q10).
- **Treating credentialed jargon as understanding.** "Self-propagating system in which people pass exams and teach others to pass exams, but nobody knows anything" (근거: Q10).
- **Surface form without underlying mechanism (cargo-cult science).** Runways, headphones, forms-of-rigor — none of it makes airplanes land (근거: Q3).
- **"Bending the truth" in your own favor — lying by omission about what could disconfirm you.** Failing to disclose details that throw doubt on your interpretation (근거: Q4).
- **Familiar-analogy explanations that smuggle in a false mechanism.** Magnets are not rubber bands; saying so is "cheating you" (근거: Q12, Q13).
- **Stopping at "why" without naming the frame of permitted assumptions.** Either you regress forever or you stop arbitrarily — both fail (근거: Q12, Q14).
- **Privileging frontier results over foundational mechanisms in plain sight.** "Nobody ever asks about a simple, ordinary phenomenon in the street" — Feynman finds the foundations where the live questions are (근거: Q18).
- **Self-criticism Feynman *himself* offers, treated as anti-pattern for the method's reach:**
  - Treating bottom-up derivation from atoms as universal pedagogy — Feynman's own *Lectures* did not work for the majority of his Caltech freshmen (근거: Q19, Q20, C3, C4).
  - Trusting "explain it simply" as a personal-completeness test rather than a community-level diagnostic. Feynman's actual claim was "*we* don't understand it," not "*you* failed" (근거: Q16, C2).
  - Treating the "Notebook of Things I Don't Know" as a study-for-the-exam tip. Even Gleick notes it "was not much use in preparing for the examination" — it reorganized Feynman's own understanding, which is a different purpose (근거: Q15, C7).

## When this thinking applies

- **잘 맞는 상황:**
  - 학습한 개념이 정말로 이해된 것인지 자기-감사할 때 (Brazil-bay 진단을 자신에게 적용) (근거: Q9, Q16).
  - 익숙한 권위·공식이 실제 메커니즘과 분리되어 있는지 확인할 때 (근거: Q5, Q17).
  - 새 문제를 공격하는데 표준 도구가 잘 안 들을 때 — 커널까지 분해해 다시 조립 (근거: Q15, Q21).
  - 추측을 시험할 *구체적 결과*를 도출할 수 있는 영역 (실험·관찰·코드 실행이 가능한 곳) (근거: Q7, Q11).
  - 자신의 설명에서 "fluent하지만 비어 있을 수 있는" 부분을 찾을 때 (freshman-lecture 시도) (근거: Q16).
- **안 맞는 상황:**
  - 복잡성 자체가 콘텐츠인 고급 주제 — 단순화가 핵심 nuance를 삭제할 때 (근거: C1, C5).
  - 학습자가 아직 분해할 *재료*가 없는 초심자 단계 — Feynman의 *Lectures*가 보통 학부생에게 잘 안 통한 이유 (근거: Q19, C3, C4).
  - 평균적 학습자에게 강의/텍스트 형태로 전달할 때 — Feynman 본인이 "piddle around with a concrete problem yourself"를 권한 이유. 방법은 능동적·고변동성·자기-구동이지 수신형이 아니다 (근거: Q20, Q21).
  - "이걸 단순화하면 거짓 친숙성이 생긴다"는 영역 — 자기장처럼 더 익숙한 무엇으로도 환원되지 않는 것들. 거기서는 reduce하지 말고 frame을 선언하라 (근거: Q12, Q13, C5).
  - 결과를 *측정·관찰·구체-사례-적용*할 방법이 없는 영역 — 세 번째 단계(reality check)가 빠지면 첫 두 단계만으로는 cargo와 구분되지 않는다 (근거: Q11, C6).

## Open tensions

- **Aggressive simplification vs. aggressive refusal to simplify.** Q1은 물리 전체를 한 문장으로 압축하고, Q12-Q13은 자석을 익숙한 비유로 설명하기를 거부한다. Feynman 본인 안에서는 이게 모순이 아니다 — *kernel-level reduction*은 메커니즘의 압축이고, *false-analogy reduction*은 없는 메커니즘의 날조다. 후자만 거부한다. 그러나 외부에서 "Feynman technique"으로 유통되는 버전은 이 구분을 종종 잃는다 (근거: Q1 vs Q12, Q13).
- **First-principles 결 vs. learning 결의 미세한 차이.** 두 결은 같은 동작이지만 적용 대상이 다르다 — first-principles는 *모르는 새 영역*을 향해 커널까지 들어가는 동작, Feynman technique은 *이미 안다고 믿는 것*을 같은 깊이로 자기-감사하는 동작. raw에는 Feynman 본인이 둘을 구분한 흔적이 없다 (Q11과 Q15가 같은 procedure의 두 적용). 그래서 essence는 묶을 수 있지만, 스킬화할 때 "어느 모드인가 — 새 문제 공격인가 자기-감사인가"를 명시하면 트리거가 명확해진다 (근거: Q11, Q15, Q21).
- **방법은 작동하는데, 가르치는 형태로는 실패한다.** Feynman 자신이 *Lectures* 서문(Q19)에서 "the system is a failure"라고 인정했다. 동시에 1-2 dozen 학생에게는 "first-rate background"가 됐다고 한다 (Q20). 즉 이 사고법은 고변동성: *맞는 사람에게는 결정적, 안 맞는 사람에게는 cargo-cult를 더 정교하게 짓는 도구가 된다.* 미해소 — 무엇이 그 두 그룹을 가르는지 Feynman은 답하지 않는다 (근거: Q19, Q20, Q21, C3, C4).
- **"Explain it simply" 테스트의 방향.** Feynman의 원본(Q16)은 *공동체 차원의 진단*("*we* don't understand")이다. 자기-감사 도구로 쓰면 작동하지만, 타인을 평가하는 잣대로 쓰면 (a) Q5-Q6의 "이름 vs. 사물" 구분을 위배하고 (b) C2가 지적한 self-help식 shaming heuristic이 된다. 같은 문장이 적용 방향에 따라 다르게 작동하는 게 미해소 긴장이다 (근거: Q16, C2).
- **간단한 설명이 *환상*을 만들 위험.** C6의 illusion-of-explanatory-depth 비판은 Feynman technique의 self-test가 "내 설명이 매끄러운가?" 가 되면 정확히 그 비판이 겨냥하는 함정에 빠진다는 점을 짚는다. Feynman의 해독제는 Q11/Q9의 "구체적 case에 적용해 *예측*이 맞는가" 이지만, 학습 모드에서는 그 case가 종종 없다. 미해소 — Feynman은 학습 모드용 reality-check를 명시하지 않았다 (근거: C6, Q9, Q11).
- **"First principles"라는 라벨 자체.** Feynman 본인의 코퍼스에서 "first principle"은 한 번, 그것도 "you must not fool yourself"를 가리키는 *epistemic posture*의 의미로 쓰였다 (Q2). Musk-style "first-principles thinking" (axioms-up reasoning)의 어휘는 Feynman에 사후 적용된 frame이다. 본 해부도는 사후 라벨이 가리키는 동작 자체는 Feynman 안에 분명히 있다고 본다 (Q1, Q11, Q15) — 그러나 *그가 그 이름으로 부르지 않았다*는 사실은 보존한다 (근거: Q2, Q11, Q15, Recurring obs.).

## Gaps inherited from raw

- **"If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough"** 은 Feynman 발화로 확인되지 않는다. 검증된 가장 가까운 원본은 Q16 (Goodstein 보고)이며, 차이는 "freshman level" vs. "simply" 와 "*we*" vs. "*you*" 다. 이 해부도는 Q16의 *공동체 진단* 의미만 사용하고, popular paraphrase는 사용하지 않았다 (raw Gaps 1).
- **"Notebook of things I don't know"의 정확한 시점·내용**은 Gleick의 2차 서술에 의존한다 (Q15). 원본 노트북은 Caltech 아카이브에 있지만 raw 단계에서 접근하지 못함. 이 해부도는 노트북을 *artifact + 사고 동작*으로만 사용하고, 그 안의 구체적 항목은 다루지 않는다 (raw Gaps 2).
- **Cargo Cult Science 원본 PDF** 접근 실패 — Q2/Q3/Q4 인용은 2차 채널을 통한 verbatim block-quote에 의존. 본 해부도는 이 인용들이 정확하다는 전제로 작성됐다. 후속 검증 필요 (raw Gaps 3).
- **"Lectures가 90% 학생에게 실패했다"의 정확한 비율**은 Feynman 본인의 정성적 표현 ("the majority of the students")이며, 후대 인용자들이 "90%"로 수치화한 것. 본 해부도는 정성적 표현만 보존했다 (raw Gaps 관련 — Q19).
- **Feynman의 learning 모드용 reality-check 절차** — 그가 *학습 자기-감사*에서 어떻게 "예측-검증" 단계를 수행했는지 1차 자료가 부족하다. Q15의 노트북은 정황만 보여주고 절차는 보여주지 않는다. Open tension의 마지막 항목과 연결됨. 추가 researcher 작업이 필요하다면 이 부분이 가장 비어 있다.
